## Abstract

This specification defines an optional extension to a tr0 CoinJoin. By default,
the taker and at least two ring-capable makers, or at least three ring-capable
makers without the taker, form one shuffled cycle of
private Taproot Lightning channels. Every CoinJoin participant keeps one equal
output. Each ring endpoint contributes its entire finalized residual across
two adjacent channels, opening one and accepting one; an ordinary maker receives
planned P2TR change instead. Each channel is genuinely funded by both endpoints
in the global CoinJoin transaction and begins with the fundee's contribution
pushed to the fundee.

The equal outputs remain the unconditional JoinMarket anonymity product. The
ring is an additional robust-connectivity layer whose privacy benefit depends on
subsequent Lightning use and closing behavior.

## Motivation

Ordinary change amounts can support cross-round wallet clustering. Moving each
ring endpoint's residual into two private channels gives those endpoints two
off-chain relationships while preserving the CoinJoin's equal-output anonymity
set. Cofunding prevents an edge from representing only one endpoint's residual,
and the cycle gives every ring endpoint the same channel role.

## Specification

### Scope and Eligibility

Let `M` be the maker count, `N = M + 1` include the taker, and `R` be the number
of ring endpoints. An explicitly selected tr0 ring MUST contain at least three
endpoints: at least two ring-capable makers when the taker joins, or at least
three ring-capable makers when the taker coordinates but does not join. Every
CoinJoin participant owns one equal output. Each ring endpoint opens one channel
to its successor and accepts one from its predecessor. The taker and other
selected makers outside the ring receive ordinary P2TR change instead of
channel outputs. A taker without an LND node can coordinate a maker-only ring.

All inputs, prevout data, equal outputs, realized maker fees, miner-fee shares,
and the feerate MUST be final before ring planning. No input or fee may be
added, removed, or adjusted within a revision after planning begins.

The current runtime requires transaction version 2, zero locktime, and
`0xffffffff` on every input. All parties commit to the exact parent before
signing. A height-based anti-fee-sniping policy is not currently implemented;
zero locktime can distinguish these rounds from otherwise comparable rounds.

An agreed rounded or equalized maker payment MAY exceed the advertised fee.
Participants MUST reject a residual below their advertised entitlement and
MUST verify that the final funding manifest preserves their exact acknowledged
contribution, including any agreed excess payment.

Each maker MUST have been selected for a `tr0` offer under JMP-0010. A ring
maker MUST additionally advertise `private_channel_ring` in the JMP-0003
feature set; an ordinary maker need not advertise it. The taker MUST understand
the extension but needs a Lightning backend only if it joins the ring. Every
ring endpoint MUST have a Lightning backend that can:

- negotiate private, final `TAPROOT` channels with external PSBT funding and
  publication disabled;
- bind a caller-supplied 32-byte pending channel ID to one channel attempt;
- expose the exact channel outpoint, capacity, endpoint balances, reserves,
  commitment fee and overhead, final `TAPROOT` type, initiator role, and private
  status to both endpoints after external PSBT verification and before either
  party authorizes a CoinJoin input signature;
- enforce the remaining negotiated policy through exact open and acceptor
  request parameters, acceptor response parameters, and bounded local policy;
  and
- retain and resume pending state across process restarts.

A backend MUST NOT advertise `private_channel_ring` merely because it has a
pending-channel listing. In particular, it MUST NOT claim complete policy
introspection when some policy fields are enforced at channel-open or
channel-accept time rather than reported in pending state.

The taker SHOULD select makers without replacement using validated fidelity-bond
weights. Bondless candidates MAY remain selectable at a configured minimal
weight. This does not replace PoDLE or any JMP-0001 admission checks.

Ring Lightning nodes MUST advertise and connect through v3 onion services over
Tor. Operators SHOULD use a dedicated Tor-only node whose identity and channels
are not reused for unrelated public activity. Node IDs and onion endpoints are
sent only in encrypted ring messages.

If the extension is not selected, participants run an ordinary JMP-0001 tr0
CoinJoin. The two flows are separate. A transaction using this extension MAY
contain ordinary tr0 makers outside the ring; each receives an exact planned
P2TR change output. The ring endpoints still form a complete directed cycle.

### Accounting and Planning

For participant `i`, indexed in cycle order modulo `N`, define `r_i` as the
participant's value remaining after its inputs, one equal output, final realized
fees, and final miner-fee share have all been accounted for. The planner chooses
strictly positive integer amounts `outgoing_i` and `incoming_i` such that:

```text
r_i = outgoing_i + incoming_i

edge i: i -> i+1
capacity_i = outgoing_i + incoming_(i+1)
push_i = incoming_(i+1)
```

`outgoing_i` is participant `i`'s gross opener contribution to edge `i`, before
the backend debits the commitment fee and any commitment-format overhead.
`incoming_i` is participant `i`'s visible fundee balance on edge `i-1`. The push
MUST equal the fundee contribution exactly. The funding output for edge `i` MUST
occur exactly once in the global unsigned transaction and have value
`capacity_i` exactly.

For each edge, the final pending accounting MUST satisfy:

```text
opener_visible_i + commitment_fee_i + commitment_overhead_i = outgoing_i
fundee_visible_i = push_i = incoming_(i+1)
opener_visible_i + fundee_visible_i
    + commitment_fee_i + commitment_overhead_i = capacity_i
```

The commitment fee and commitment overhead belong entirely to the opener side;
neither is a separate participant contribution or transaction output. A backend
adapter MUST provide a conservative bounded overhead value to planning and MUST
verify the actual value from the two endpoint observations. The protocol does
not fix that value forever. The joinmarket-ng patched-LND v0.21.3-beta final-Taproot
profile currently defaults to, and live measurements verify, 660 satoshis for
the two anchors. Other backend versions MUST advertise and verify their own
value without weakening local capacity, fee, reserve, or spendable-balance
limits.

The taker MUST use an operating-system CSPRNG to choose both a uniformly shuffled
cycle and randomized splits. It MUST use rejection sampling until every edge
satisfies the two backends' channel minimum and maximum, push limit, dust limit,
reserve, CSV-delay, and commitment-fee constraints. At minimum, the exact
post-open state MUST leave:

```text
outgoing_i >= opener_required_reserve_i
              + maximum_commitment_fee_i
              + commitment_overhead_i
              + spendable_margin_i
incoming_(i+1) >= fundee_required_reserve_i
                 + spendable_margin_(i+1)
```

The planner MUST also keep the fundee contribution above the endpoint dust
limit. Maximum commitment fees, overhead, reserves, and margins are conservative
planning limits; exact balances, the commitment fee, and overhead are checked
again from both endpoints after PSBT verification.

The split sampler is a privacy parameter, not only a feasibility one. Drawing
each `outgoing_i` in cycle order from its own feasible interval is simple and
satisfies the constraints above, but it is not uniform over the joint feasible
region, so an observer who knows the sampler can rank candidate splits rather
than treat them as equally likely. Because every ring endpoint's residual is
fixed by its private plan, this affects how well a split resists a Bayesian
observer, not whether the amounts are recoverable outright. Implementations
SHOULD document the distribution they sample from, and MAY use a sampler that
is uniform over the feasible region.

Each ring endpoint MUST independently recompute `r_i` from the fixed transaction
terms and verify `outgoing_i + incoming_i = r_i` before acknowledging a plan and
again before signing readiness. It MUST reject zero, negative, out-of-range, or
non-integer amounts.

Every candidate in a revision MUST satisfy every eligibility and feasibility
check. Any failure cancels the complete revision. A new revision is allowed only
after every ring endpoint confirms safe retirement; otherwise the attempt remains
in recovery. A new revision uses the same round nonce with a strictly greater
revision and fresh pending channel IDs, plans, backend attempts, ring keys, and
signatures. No unresolved verified revision may coexist with a replacement
revision for the same round.

### Channel Policy and Inbound Matching

Every edge MUST negotiate a final `TAPROOT` commitment type, remain private and
unannounced, require confirmation before use, enable SCID alias, and disable
zero-conf and backend publication of the funding transaction. Every plan and
manifest uses this policy contract (alias is a mandatory backend invariant,
not an extra field in the signed policy object):

```json
{
  "commitment_type": "TAPROOT",
  "fundee_csv_delay": 144,
  "fundee_reserve": 10000,
  "min_depth": 3,
  "opener_csv_delay": 144,
  "opener_reserve": 10000,
  "private": true,
  "zero_conf": false
}
```

CSV delays MUST be integers from 1 through 2016, `min_depth` from 1 through 144,
and reserves within the satoshi bounds below. No other commitment type or
boolean value is valid. Both endpoints MUST agree to this object exactly. This
object is a signed contract, not a claim that every field is later returned by
the backend's pending-channel API.

Before allowing an incoming attempt, the fundee receives its expected predecessor
and edge parameters from the taker over the encrypted JoinMarket session. It MUST
match the backend event against all of the following:

- opener Lightning node ID;
- `round_nonce`, `revision`, and `pending_channel_id`;
- chain hash, exact capacity, and initial push;
- final `TAPROOT`, private, zero-conf-disabled, and SCID-alias-enabled flags;
- fundee reserve and fundee CSV delay; and
- bounded dust, fee-rate, HTLC, value-in-flight, and accepted-HTLC fields.

On acceptance, the fundee MUST return the planned opener reserve, opener CSV
delay, and minimum confirmation depth in its exact acceptor response. The opener
MUST send the planned capacity, push, private/final-Taproot flags, fundee reserve,
both CSV limits, caller-supplied pending ID, and `no_publish=true` in its exact
open request. These request/response checks and local bounds enforce policy
fields that are not all available later through `PendingChannels`.

The fundee MUST reject unsolicited or mismatched attempts. Matching only amount
or peer identity is insufficient. Each `pending_channel_id` MUST be a fresh
uniformly random 32-byte value, unique across all edges and all revisions known
to the implementation.

A ring endpoint MUST NOT acknowledge its plan until its backend has actually
established the acceptor, not merely scheduled it. Acknowledging earlier lets the
predecessor open while the acceptor is still inactive, in which case the backend
judges the inbound channel by its own default policy instead of the negotiated
contract, and the mismatch is only caught later during pending observation.

### PSBT Funding and Readiness

After every ring endpoint has acknowledged its exact local plan and armed its
incoming acceptor, the taker sends `ring_open`. Each ring endpoint then starts its
one outgoing externally funded channel with `no_publish=true` and the assigned
pending channel ID while accepting its one incoming attempt. Before a global
PSBT exists, only the opener receives the negotiated funding scriptPubKey. The
fundee's acceptor observation contains the pending ID, opener node ID, capacity,
and push. It remains bound to the separately validated planned policy contract,
but does not contain the funding script.

Immediately before invoking the backend open RPC, each ring endpoint MUST durably
record that its outgoing attempt started. This boundary distinguishes a plan
that was acknowledged but never opened from an attempt that may have reached the
backend before a crash. Cancellation MUST skip the shim RPC only when this record
proves the outgoing attempt never started; otherwise an unknown shim remains
ambiguous and requires recovery.

Each `ring_prepared` therefore contains a `PreparedOutgoingState` with the
funding script and a separate `PreparedIncomingState` without it. The taker MUST
cross-check every outgoing report with the successor's reciprocal incoming
observation for all shared fields, and MUST reject duplicate funding scripts.

The taker then constructs one global unsigned transaction and PSBT from the
already fixed CoinJoin inputs and outputs. Every participant has one equal
output, and every negotiated channel scriptPubKey occurs once at its exact
capacity. Duplicate scripts are forbidden even if a backend returns one. Output
ordering MUST be fixed before readiness.

For its outgoing edge, every opener MUST call its backend's equivalent of
`PsbtVerify` with the global PSBT, its pending channel ID, and
`skip_finalize=true`. It MUST NOT ask the backend to publish. `PsbtVerify`
consumes the opener's funding shim, so the opener MUST durably record this
boundary before performing subsequent pending-state observations. The fundee
MUST independently inspect backend state tied to the same unsigned transaction
and output. Stock LND v0.21.1 live tests show that after this call both endpoints
can observe and cross-check these exact pending values:

- displayed channel point (`unsigned_txid:output_index`) and capacity;
- reciprocal local and remote balances;
- local and remote reserves, commitment fee, and commitment overhead;
- final `TAPROOT` type, local/remote initiator role, and private status; and
- the manifest funding output, unsigned transaction hashes, pending channel ID,
  nonce, and revision to which the participant had already bound the backend.

The funding script is checked against the exact manifest output and opener's
prepared negotiation. The pending-channel listing need not expose the pending
ID or every policy field; the prior exact open/acceptor checks remain part of the
signed readiness contract. Missing policy introspection MUST NOT be represented
as an observed value.

Each ring endpoint sends exactly two `ring_ready` messages, one for its outgoing
opener endpoint and one for its incoming fundee endpoint. Every edge therefore
has exactly two readiness signatures. For `R` ring endpoints, the taker MUST
validate all `2R` private
states and endpoint signatures, including reciprocal endpoint equality, before
constructing the complete `ring_ready_set`.

Every ring maker MUST verify and durably store the manifest and all `2R` signatures,
then return `ring_ready_set_ack` over the exact readiness-set hash. This is the
implemented explicit ring-ready ACK; `ring_ready_ack` is not a separate wire
type. Only after every ring maker ACK is validated may the taker send `ring_sign`.
Each ring maker MUST durably enter `SIGNING` for the exact manifest and unsigned
transaction before returning `ring_sign_ack`. The taker MUST collect every ring
maker's exact `ring_sign_ack` before sending the ordinary `!tx`; all makers then
return ordinary `!sig`. No backend or participant may publish a different
funding transaction. Only the taker broadcasts the exact final transaction.

### Manifest and Signatures

Every ring endpoint and the taker coordinator generate fresh BIP340 key pairs
from a CSPRNG for each round
revision. Ring keys MUST NOT be derived from wallet, bond, JoinMarket identity,
or Lightning node keys and MUST never be reused. The encrypted session binds a
maker's ring key to its already authenticated JoinMarket session.

The public manifest contains only data visible in the unsigned transaction or
needed to authenticate the ring:

```json
{
  "edges": [{
    "acceptor_key": "<32-byte x-only hex>",
    "capacity": 400000,
    "opener_key": "<32-byte x-only hex>",
    "output_index": 8,
    "pending_channel_id": "<32-byte hex>",
    "policy": {"commitment_type": "TAPROOT", "fundee_csv_delay": 144,
               "fundee_reserve": 10000, "min_depth": 3,
               "opener_csv_delay": 144, "opener_reserve": 10000,
               "private": true, "zero_conf": false},
    "script_pubkey": "<hex>"
  }],
  "equal_output_indices": [0, 2, 4, 6],
  "network": "signet",
  "outputs": [{"amount": 100000, "index": 0, "script_pubkey": "<hex>"}],
  "participant_keys": ["<32-byte x-only hex>"],
  "revision": 0,
  "round_nonce": "<32-byte hex>",
  "unsigned_tx_hash": "<32-byte hex>",
  "unsigned_txid": "<display-order txid hex>",
  "payload_format": "canonical_json"
}
```

Arrays contain all entries in transaction or cycle order. The manifest MUST
contain `R` ring endpoint keys, `R` edges, `N` equal-output indices, and all
`2N` transaction outputs exactly once, including `N - R` ordinary P2TR change
outputs. An edge's opener and acceptor keys MUST be
adjacent in cycle order. It MUST NOT contain any Lightning node ID, onion
endpoint, `r_i`, `outgoing_i`, `incoming_i`, push, wallet identity, or mapping
from a participant to its equal output. Non-neighbors therefore learn topology
only through ephemeral ring keys and public output data.

A readiness state body contains `pending_channel_id`, endpoint ring keys,
funding script, capacity, opener and fundee balances, push, both reserves,
commitment fee, commitment overhead, policy, endpoint role and signer key,
nonce, revision, manifest hash, unsigned transaction hash and ID, output index,
and a fresh 32-byte `state_salt`. Observed fields MUST come from the endpoint;
contract fields MUST remain bound to the exact earlier plan and backend request.
The body is encrypted to the taker and is not placed in the manifest. Its public
attestation is:

```json
{
  "edge": "<pending_channel_id>",
  "manifest_hash": "<32-byte hex>",
  "revision": 0,
  "role": "opener",
  "round_nonce": "<32-byte hex>",
  "signer_key": "<32-byte x-only hex>",
  "state_hash": "<32-byte hex>"
}
```

The salt prevents practical enumeration of private split amounts from
`state_hash`. The taker verifies the full state body; other participants verify
the endpoint's signature over its attestation.

### Wire Encoding

Extension messages use this envelope inside the existing encrypted JoinMarket
message channel:

```text
!ring <type> <base64url-no-padding(canonical_json(payload))>
```

`canonical_json` is UTF-8 JSON Canonicalization Scheme (RFC 8785). Every schema
string is ASCII, so this equals JSON with lexicographically sorted keys, no
insignificant whitespace, and no escapes beyond those JSON requires. Nesting
depth MUST NOT exceed 10. Schemas use
integers for satoshis and counters, lowercase hex without `0x`, no `null`, no
duplicate keys, and no unrecognized fields. Decoded payloads MUST be at most
65,536 bytes. `R` and `N` MUST each be between 3 and 32; satoshi values MUST be between 0 and
2,100,000,000,000,000; revisions MUST be between 0 and 2,147,483,647. Nonces,
hashes, pending IDs, and salts are exactly 64 hex characters; x-only keys are
64; compressed node IDs are 66; scriptPubKeys are at most 10,000 hex characters;
and onion endpoints are at most 320 ASCII characters. A parser MUST reject a
payload outside these bounds before allocating nested structures.

Every payload contains the literal fields `payload_format: "canonical_json"`,
`type`, `round_nonce`, `revision`,
`signer_key`, and `sig`. The following type-specific fields define the state
machine:

| Type | Direction | Required type-specific content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ring_invite` | taker to maker | `network`, `offer_type`, `expiry`, `policy_bounds` |
| `ring_hello` | participant to taker | `participant` |
| `ring_plan` | taker to participant | `network`, `offer_type`, `cycle_keys`, `position`, `contribution`, `predecessor`, `successor`, `incoming_edge`, `outgoing_edge` |
| `ring_plan_ack` | participant to taker | `plan_hash` |
| `ring_open` | taker to participant | `plan_hash` |
| `ring_prepared` | participant to taker | `outgoing`, `incoming` |
| `ring_unsigned` | taker to participants | `unsigned_tx`, `psbt`, `manifest` |
| `ring_ready` | participant to taker, exactly two each | `state`, `attestation`, `endpoint_signature` |
| `ring_ready_set` | taker to participants | `manifest`, `attestations` |
| `ring_ready_set_ack` | maker to taker | `readiness_hash` |
| `ring_sign` | taker to participants | `manifest_hash`, `unsigned_tx_hash` |
| `ring_sign_ack` | maker to taker | `manifest_hash`, `unsigned_tx_hash` |
| `ring_cancel` | either direction | `reason_code`, `canceled_pending_ids` |

`ring_prepared.outgoing` contains `pending_channel_id`, `opener_key`,
`acceptor_key`, `script_pubkey`, `capacity`, `push_amount`, and `policy`.
`ring_prepared.incoming` replaces `script_pubkey` with `opener_node_id`. All
payloads, including `ring_invite`, are signed by `signer_key`; `sig` is omitted
only while computing its own payload signature. BIP340 signatures use the
revision's ring key and these exact 32-byte messages:

```text
H_object(type, object) = SHA256(
    UTF8("JMP0014/" + type + "/v1\u0000") || canonical_json(object)
)
H_payload(type, payload_without_top_level_sig) = SHA256(
    UTF8("JMP0014/" + type + "/canonical_json\u0000")
    || canonical_json(payload_without_top_level_sig)
)

manifest_hash = H_object("manifest", manifest)
plan_hash = H_object("plan", signed_ring_plan_payload)
state_hash = H_object("ready-state", readiness_state_body)
readiness_hash = H_object("ready-set", signed_readiness_attestations)
endpoint_signature = BIP340_sign(ring_secret_key, H_object("ready", attestation))
sig = BIP340_sign(ring_secret_key, H_payload(type, payload_without_top_level_sig))
```

The NUL is one byte `00`; concatenation has no length prefix or newline. Only
the payload's top-level `sig` is omitted; nested signatures remain committed.
Fixed canonical-byte, payload-digest, and signature vectors are tested in
`jmcore/tests/test_cofunded_ring.py`. The
payload's `type` value MUST equal the envelope type. `unsigned_tx_hash` is
single SHA256 of the consensus serialization without witness; `unsigned_txid`
is the conventional display-order double-SHA256 transaction ID. PSBT bytes are
standard binary PSBT encoded with padded base64 inside the JSON string. These
rules fully determine all hashing and signature inputs.

### State Machine and Cancellation

The durable implementation progression is `INVITED`, `PLANNED`,
`ACCEPTOR_ARMED`, `PREPARED`, `PSBT_VERIFIED`, `READY`, `SIGNING`, `SIGNED`,
`BROADCAST`, then `CONFIRMED_OPEN`. Retirement uses `RETIRING` then `RETIRED`;
unsafe or ambiguous states use `RECOVERY_REQUIRED`, and only a confirmed
conflicting spend permits `CONFLICTED`. These states are local: this revision
defines no status message, because every ring endpoint resolves the exact
funding transaction against its own chain backend and MUST NOT treat a peer's
report as evidence. Messages received out of order, for another nonce or
revision, or after a terminal state MUST be rejected. Repeated identical messages MAY return a durably cached response;
conflicting repeats MUST abort while safe cancellation remains possible.

Cancellation has exactly two safe paths:

1. Before local `PsbtVerify`, a participant cancels only its outgoing opener
   shim and cancels its incoming acceptor task. It MUST NOT try to cancel its
   predecessor's shim; that opener owns it. A backend reporting the shim already
   absent proves cancellation only when the same process created that shim and
   knows verification never ran. A shim restored after a restart is ambiguous,
   because a backend reports the same absence for a shim that `PsbtVerify`
   consumed, so that case MUST enter `RECOVERY_REQUIRED` rather than `RETIRED`
   unless durable local evidence of the verification and its exact observed
   channel point was restored with it.
2. After local `PsbtVerify`, shim cancellation is invalid because verification
   consumed the shim. Before `ring_sign`, any local CoinJoin input signature,
   final transaction observation, or broadcast, the participant MAY retire only
   its two exact verified external channel points. It MUST first prove the exact
   unsigned TXID absent from both mempool and confirmed chain and prove every
   local CoinJoin input still unspent. It may then call LND `AbandonChannel` for
   each exact point with `pending_funding_shim_only=false` and the dangerous
   `i_know_what_i_am_doing=true` override, and MUST verify the point disappeared
   from every pending-channel category.

The second path is destructive and MUST NOT run if signing, publication, final
transaction observation, input status, mempool status, chain status, channel
identity, or the postcondition is uncertain. Failure enters
`RECOVERY_REQUIRED`; it is not success. This guarded flow passed the two-node
live test at both endpoints, and a second fresh verified open
proved the channel slots reusable.

Transmission of `ring_sign` is the point of no cancellation, even if an ACK is
lost. Participants MUST durably store the manifest, PSBT, exact pending state,
complete readiness set, signing authorization, local signatures, and final
transaction at their respective transition boundaries. After `ring_sign` or any
input signature, implementations MUST NOT abandon, cancel, revise, or substitute
the funding transaction. They retain state until the exact transaction confirms
or a confirmed conflicting spend makes it impossible.

The taker broadcasts only after collecting and validating every input signature.
If broadcast is uncertain or the exact transaction leaves the mempool,
participants with the durably stored final transaction rebroadcast only those
exact bytes. They MUST NOT alter inputs, outputs, ordering, locktime, or sequence
values. Fee acceleration MAY use descendants that do not change the funding
transaction. An absent exact transaction with any spent local input is ambiguous
until a conflicting spend is confirmed and MUST enter reconciliation or recovery,
not cancellation. A confirmed conflicting input spend moves the revision to
`CONFLICTED`; records remain retained, and cleanup MUST use only the exact
confirmed-conflict evidence and backend-defined recovery.

## Compatibility

The capability and all `!ring` messages are optional. Peers that do not
advertise `private_channel_ring` are not candidates for a ring attempt. A
ring-enabled taker MUST filter ring candidates for the exact feature and
configured `tr0` offer family before `!fill`; ordinary makers need a compatible
`tr0` offer but not the feature. Replacements during fill or authentication MUST
satisfy their selected role's filter. Once `ring_invite` is sent, the ring and
ordinary maker roles
are immutable: there is no maker replacement, smaller ring, change of a maker's
role, or ordinary CoinJoin fallback in that attempt. An ordinary maker already
selected before the invite retains its planned P2TR change. Any failure before
signing authorization aborts and requires the applicable safe retirement path;
after authorization the exact transaction remains retained for signing,
broadcast, and reconciliation. A separately initiated attempt may omit the
extension. Existing JMP-0001 and JMP-0010 message schemas are unchanged except
for the `!ioauth` input hold below; after both ring ACK barriers, their normal
`!tx` and `!sig` flow carries the exact authorized transaction.

### Input Hold in `!ioauth`

A maker appends one field to its encrypted `!ioauth` plaintext:

```text
<utxo_list> <auth_pub> <cj_addr> <change_addr> <btc_sig> <hold_seconds>
```

`hold_seconds` is how long the maker keeps its inputs reserved for this taker
while waiting for `!tx`: a decimal integer from 0 to 3600 without leading zeros.
The taker measures it from receipt of `!ioauth`. Ordinary makers send it too,
because a ring round keeps every selected input reserved until channel setup
completes. A ring-enabled taker MUST reject an `!ioauth` without this field and
MUST abort before `ring_invite` when any selected maker's hold cannot cover the
setup deadline plus its safety margin. Outside ring rounds, takers MUST accept
both the legacy five-field and the six-field form; the reference implementation
parses fields positionally and ignores the extra one.

### Anti-Grief and Persistence

Implementations MUST configure hard limits for active ring sessions and the
subset with verified but unresolved PSBT state. The verified limit MUST NOT
exceed the active limit. Reaching either limit blocks new invitations rather
than evicting retained state. The current joinmarket-ng defaults are four active
sessions and two verified sessions per local participant store. An input outpoint
MUST NOT belong to two active sessions, and unresolved verified state MUST block
a higher revision for the same participant and round.

Private participant state MUST be atomically and durably stored with restrictive
permissions before each externally visible ACK or signature. It includes fresh
ring secrets, plans, exact backend attempts, pending IDs and points, PSBT and
manifest, readiness records and ACKs, signatures, final transaction, chain
evidence, and idempotent request/response records. A restart MUST re-arm an
accepted-but-not-observed inbound attempt where safe, restore exact incoming and
outgoing backend expectations, and resume observation without negotiating a
substitute transaction. `SIGNED` or `BROADCAST` state with exact final bytes MUST
rebroadcast only those bytes when absent. A state that cannot be resumed safely
MUST enter `RECOVERY_REQUIRED`.

Malformed, permission-unsafe, identity-inconsistent, or otherwise corrupt
records MUST be reported and retained. Corruption blocks new records and normal
reconciliation; an implementation MUST NOT silently delete, overwrite, or route
around evidence to free a session slot.

## Reference Implementation

The tested joinmarket-ng branch uses these conceptual module paths. Links point
to the corresponding public repository areas because the files are not yet on
the public `main` branch:

- [`jmcore/cofunded_ring.py`](https://github.com/joinmarket-ng/joinmarket-ng/tree/main/jmcore/src/jmcore): strict schemas, canonical encoding, hashes, and signatures;

Live regtest tests cover a participating taker, three ring makers and an
ordinary maker in one five-party CoinJoin, and a separate maker-only ring whose
taker has no LND node. The mixed round includes PoDLE and fidelity-bond rentals,
private final-Taproot channels, restart reconciliation and three buyout variants
(public route, direct private peer and failed-payment CSV split). The external
funding tests cover guarded post-verification abandonment and slot reuse. These
tests use a locally patched LND and do not establish mainnet deployment,
cross-implementation interoperability, maker restart at every protocol phase,
or every confirmed-conflict recovery path. JMP-0014 therefore remains Draft.

## Privacy and Security Considerations

The requirement for one equal output per participant is unconditional; the
anonymity of ownership is not. The ring adds connectivity and may make residual
ownership less direct, but it does not weaken the requirement for equal outputs
or replace fidelity-bond-weighted maker selection.

Channel privacy is conditional. Participants SHOULD perform economically real,
independently timed payments, routing, swaps, or rebalancing before closing and
SHOULD stagger cooperative closes. Mechanical circular transfers are not a
substitute for real activity. A cooperative close at unchanged balances can
reveal the original allocation, while correlated or simultaneous closes aid
cohort analysis. A force close is especially distinctive and can tag the ring
cohort through its commitment transaction and timing.

Each neighbor necessarily learns the other's Lightning node ID, the shared
channel point, and the local allocation on that edge. The taker knows the entire
topology, every node ID, and every private contribution. Other participants see
ephemeral topology and public outputs but not non-neighbor node identities or
split amounts. A malicious taker can exploit this knowledge, so dedicated
Tor-only nodes, fresh ring keys, and fidelity-bond weighting are material
protections rather than implementation details.

The funding output and any later close are on chain. This construction does not
justify a claim that a participant has no on-chain successor. Its benefit arises
only when private channel use changes balances and cooperative closes are
staggered. Readiness signatures prevent input signing against unverified pending
state, but they do not guarantee future liveness, routing demand, cooperative
closure, or protection from a backend defect.

Evaluation must distinguish a transaction's equal-output count from ambiguity
in the complete funding and spending graph. Independent amount-partition and
cross-round analysis should include unchanged balances, colluding neighbors,
reused Lightning identities, force-close cohorts, and later consolidation.
A successful funding or payment test does not establish resistance to these
observers or quantify an anonymity gain.
