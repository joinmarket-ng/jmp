# JMP-0013: Signed Market Faults

## Abstract

This document defines two objectively verifiable faults for the experimental
[JMP-0012 market](jmp-0012.md): conflicting finalized allocations and a signed
delivery containing a statically invalid credential. A valid proof excludes the
responsible bond from upgraded clients' ordinary CoinJoin selection for a fixed
interval. No response from the accused seller is necessary to reject fabricated
evidence. The mechanism is an exclusion of earning opportunities, not seizure,
onchain slashing, or escrow.

## Motivation

A buyer must not be able to turn a lost message, payment preimage, unrelated
transaction, or forged assertion into a bond blacklist entry. Conversely, an
owner that expressly signs accountability terms must not escape a provable
contradiction by changing its online market key or maker nick.

## Specification

### Authority and Canonical Records

JMP-0012 encoding, size bounds, period definitions, and signatures apply. Only a
bond owner's valid `authorization` with `policy: signed-faults-v1` delegates
accountability. Ordinary fidelity-bond certificates authorize **neither** market
sales **nor** punitive statements. The owner should keep its market key distinct
from every renter's certificate key.

A proof is an object containing `kind: fault`, integer `version: 1`, `reason`,
`authorization` (owner-signed), `first` (seller-signed allocation), `second`, and
nullable `second_authorization`. The entire canonical proof is at most 16,384
bytes. It is broadcast as `!mproof <base64 canonical proof>` through the same
ephemeral-identity, isolated-circuit pattern used for commitment gossip.

The reporter's identity is irrelevant to proof validity. Reporters need not
prove they paid, and possession of a preimage does not authenticate them. They
must instead present the **seller's own signed finalized statements**.

### Double Allocation

For `reason: double-allocation`, `second` is another finalized allocation.
`second_authorization`, if present, supplies its owner authorization; otherwise
the first authorization applies to both records.

The verifier MUST check both owner signatures, both seller signatures, each
authorization-body reference, and each allocation schema and product binding.
Both authorities MUST identify exactly the same network, bond outpoint, bond
public key, and locktime. Both allocations MUST identify the same product and
resource.

For bond rentals, the authorization periods MUST match: distinct rental periods
are not conflicting resources. For PoDLE, a repeat allocation of the same
commitment remains a conflict across periods; the later authorized period is
the fault period. Changing a market signing key does not remove liability.

The allocations conflict when their `(allocation_id, buyer_tag,
certificate_pubkey)` tuples differ. Re-signing an identical allocation is not
evidence. Reusing the same allocation ID for different buyers **is** evidence.
Two unpaid quotes, two inquiries, or two ordinary legacy certificates are not
this proof. Finalized allocation IDs are irreversible and retries MUST return
the original allocation, not create a replacement receipt for the same buyer.

Public double-allocation proofs disclose resource hashes and buyer tags, not
PoDLE openings, invoices, onchain payment outputs, or private keys. Buyer tags are
trade-local pseudonyms; publishing evidence still has a privacy cost and must be
an explicit user operation.

### Invalid Delivery

For `reason: invalid-delivery`, `second` is a seller-signed `delivery` record and
`second_authorization` MUST be null. The delivery's allocation reference MUST
match the first allocation body hash. The original authorization and allocation
must themselves be valid; unrelated malformed bytes are not evidence.

After signature verification, the credential is checked deterministically:

* PoDLE schema, exact declared index proof, script-to-public-key binding,
  commitment-to-resource equality, network equality, and the version-1 market
  retry-index range of 0 through 2.
* Bond credential schema, exact bond reference, requested renter certificate
  public key, legacy certificate signature, and expiry at `period+1`.

Failure of one of these static checks constitutes the fault. An unavailable
backend, spent output, reorganized block, changed confirmation count, stale
metadata on a different chain view, market price disagreement, or transaction
failure does **not**. Those conditions require chain context or causal/timing
claims that this evidence language deliberately does not assert.

The buyer cannot substitute arbitrary plaintext: the exact invalid credential
must carry the authorized seller's signature. Revealing an encryption key alone
is not sufficient. A public invalid-delivery proof may reveal a PoDLE opening;
the reporter SHOULD treat that opening as permanently exposed and unusable.
Other malformed credential fields may contain arbitrary seller-supplied private
information. Users MUST inspect evidence before publication; implementations MUST
NOT automatically publish decrypted deliveries as fault evidence.

### Applying a Sanction

Let `p` be the fault period. Exclusion applies through the end of period `p+1`,
inclusive, and never before period `p`. Covering the following full period avoids
reducing the penalty to almost zero for a fault near a boundary. Replay does not
restart or extend the interval. Clients calculate periods from their own verified
Bitcoin chain height, not a reporter's timestamp or claimed tip.

A proof authorizes no blockchain lookup by itself. A malicious signer can put
an arbitrary outpoint next to its own key in a self-signed document. Clients
MUST match the complete bond tuple to an independently verified ordinary bond
claim before excluding a maker. An outpoint-only match is unsafe: it would let
an attacker name a victim's outpoint while signing with its own key.

Every offer associated with that verified bond is excluded, including multiple
nicks and zero-fee offers. Implementations MUST NOT merely set bond weight to
zero, or place the nick in a soft preference list that is relaxed when liquidity
is thin. All regular, sweep, and replacement selection paths must retain the
hard exclusion. An unrelated bond, unrelated renter certificate, or unbonded
identity cannot be excluded solely because of an alleged association.

A dishonest seller can still discard its identity and participate without the
bond, or use another bond. The protocol does not attempt to create permanent
real-world identity tracking. An innocent renter of a sanctioned bond may also
lose selections; buyers should check known evidence before renting and should
not treat a lease as guaranteeing future earning power.

### Dissemination and Resource Bounds

Verification is local, not a directory vote or an accusation count. No number of
unverified reports creates evidence. Clients bound decoded size, queues, parser
depth, signature work, negative caches, and unverified chain claims. The reference
implementation captures at most 64 pending proofs per directory client and
admits at most eight cryptographic verifications per second per fault cache.

At most 256 distinct bond-period resources are cached. Unbacked claims MUST NOT
evict promoted evidence that has already matched an independently verified bond.
Only such promoted proofs are eligible for private durable storage. A corrupted
cache or unavailable chain view must not fabricate a sanction. Persistence is
not a global blacklist authority; signatures are rechecked when loading.

Gossip is best-effort and has no consensus, ordering, or availability guarantee.
Partitions, offline clients, censorship, and saturation can delay evidence or
make observers disagree temporarily. Legacy clients do not apply these sanctions.
A buyer MAY retain and rebroadcast its original proof without extending its
fixed validity interval. This version does not add a globally replicated proof
database or claim that a missing proof establishes an honest seller.

## Rationale

No interactive defense timeout is used. Requiring an innocent seller to come
online and rebut an accusation would make network partitions and liveness bugs
into a slashing mechanism. An invalid accusation fails locally even if the seller
never sees it. A valid pair of conflicting signatures cannot be undone by a later
denial; only a verifier bug or a genuinely compromised authorized signing key can
invalidate the security assumptions.

This intentionally leaves non-delivery after payment unpunishable. An onchain
transaction or valid invoice preimage proves neither who received a response nor
which side interrupted communication. Eliminating that risk requires a different
fair-exchange construction, escrow, or additional trust, not weaker evidence
rules. Signed refunds and operator reconciliation do not retroactively turn
timeouts into objective faults.

## Security Considerations

The stake at risk is future use of a public bond in participating clients, not
its principal. The loss depends on demand, remaining locktime, adoption of this
extension, and how much the owner values maker/rental income. An idle or cheap
bond may have little deterrent value. There is no protocol-enforced rental price
floor, guaranteed compensation, or proof that a collateral amount makes a trade
economically safe. Users must cap exposure and understand this limitation.

Signing-key compromise can create authentic conflicting receipts. Separating the
market key from renter keys and limiting authorizations to one period bounds but
does not eliminate that liability. Operators must preserve issuance tombstones
across restores and avoid running uncoordinated sellers against copied inventory.

Malformed, cross-network, wrong-key, expired, unrelated, duplicate, and reordered
evidence must be tested. Tests must also cover changing buyer tags under one
allocation ID, distinct authorized seller keys, PoDLE resale across periods,
honest quote expiry, and hard exclusion under insufficient liquidity.

## Backward Compatibility

No existing `!hp2` meaning or fidelity-bond cryptographic format changes. Public
market evidence has a separate command and signature domain. Legacy peers ignore
it. Upgraded takers evaluate valid evidence even when they do not themselves buy
or sell market credentials, because the responsible owner explicitly opted into
the policy.

## Reference Implementation

Experimental JoinMarket NG branch `feat/privacy-credential-market` implements
the signed evidence language in `jmcore.credential_market`, bounded handling in
`jmcore.market_faults`, and hard filtering before ordinary taker offer selection.
Unit and regtest tests exercise real signatures and credential use.
