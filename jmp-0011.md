## Abstract

This specification defines `channel_buyout_v1`. A JoinMarket participant that
owns a private Taproot Lightning channel spends the channel funding output as
an ordinary P2TR input of a tr0 CoinJoin, alone. Its channel counterparty is
not a CoinJoin participant and is unknown to the other participants. The
participant's CoinJoin change is a Taproot escrow output shared with the
counterparty: a MuSig2 key path for cooperative spends, one script leaf that
lets the participant claim the whole output with a payment preimage, and a
presigned relative-timelocked split transaction that pays the counterparty its
share if the participant never pays. After the CoinJoin confirms, the
participant buys out the counterparty's share with a Lightning payment; the
payment preimage unlocks the escrow.

The CoinJoin protocol of JMP-0001 and JMP-0010 is not changed. All new
messages travel between the two channel endpoints over their authenticated
Lightning peer connection.

## Motivation

A channel close normally produces a transaction whose shape (one 2-of-2 input,
two outputs) and timing tag both endpoints and link their subsequent wallets.
Spending the funding output inside a tr0 CoinJoin instead gives the channel
owner an equal output in a large anonymity set and turns the close into an
ordinary-looking key-path input, because a tr0 round contains only P2TR
key-path inputs and P2TR outputs.

Requiring the counterparty to join the same round, or to be a maker, would
force the counterparty to reveal itself to the coordinator and would make the
round's structure depend on channel relationships. This design keeps the
counterparty off the CoinJoin entirely. It only cosigns the exact transaction,
receives a trustless on-chain fallback, and is paid over Lightning.

## Scope and Roles

- The **buyer** `B` is a JoinMarket participant (taker or maker) in a tr0
  round and one endpoint of one or more eligible channels.
- The **counterparty** `C` is the other endpoint of those channels. It runs a
  buyout-capable Lightning backend but no JoinMarket software is required of it.
- The **round peers** are the taker and makers of the round other than `B`.
  They receive no message defined here and MUST be unable to distinguish a
  buyout round from an ordinary tr0 round by protocol data.

A round MAY contain more than one buyer; buyers are independent and need not
know about each other. The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, and MAY are to be
interpreted as in RFC 2119.

## Protocol Overview

1. `B` selects a channel, proposes a buyout to `C`, and `C` accepts with fresh
   escrow material and its frozen entitlement. Both freeze the channel.
2. `B` enters the tr0 round as an ordinary participant. Its input set contains
   the channel funding outpoints plus at least one ordinary key-path input. Its
   change address is the escrow output script.
3. When the exact unsigned CoinJoin is known, `B` sends it to `C`. Both
   compute the escrow outpoint, build the split transaction, and sign it with
   MuSig2. Only when `C` holds a complete split signature does it release its
   partial signatures for the channel inputs.
4. `B` aggregates the channel-input signatures and returns them with its
   ordinary `!sig` messages. The taker broadcasts as usual.
5. After the CoinJoin reaches the agreed depth, `C` issues an invoice for its
   claim; `B` pays it, learns the preimage, and sweeps the escrow, normally by
   a cooperative key-path spend and otherwise by the preimage leaf, before the
   split becomes valid.
6. If `B` never pays, `C` broadcasts the presigned split once its relative
   timelock matures and receives its claim plus an agreed compensation.

## Eligibility

A channel is eligible when all of the following hold:

- Its funding output is P2TR spent by a MuSig2 key path (a `SIMPLE_TAPROOT`
  channel in current backends). Non-Taproot channels are not eligible.
- It is private and was never announced. Its funding outpoint MUST NOT appear
  in public gossip.
- It has no pending HTLCs and is quiesced (BOLT 2 `stfu` or an equivalent
  backend freeze) so that the commitment balances cannot change.
- Neither endpoint has a pending or in-progress close, splice, or other
  funding-output spend.
- The chain hash matches the round's network.

`B` MAY spend several channels in one round only when they all have the same
counterparty `C` and the accounting below is satisfied for their sum. Channels
with different counterparties MUST NOT be combined in one round, because the
round has exactly one escrow change output per buyer.

`B` MUST contribute at least one ordinary BIP86 key-path P2TR input of its own
in the same round. A taker uses it for the JMP-0010 PoDLE commitment and a
maker uses it as the `!ioauth` authentication key; neither role can be filled
by a funding output whose key is a two-party aggregate. `B` MAY contribute
further ordinary inputs.

## Accounting

Let `cap_k` be the capacity of channel `k`. From the quiesced commitment state,
both endpoints compute `C`'s **entitlement**:

```text
entitlement_C_k = to_C_k + (commit_fee_k + anchors_k if C is the initiator of k)
entitlement_C   = sum_k entitlement_C_k
```

`to_C_k` is `C`'s commitment balance. The commitment fee and both anchor
amounts belong to the channel initiator, exactly as in a cooperative close. Both
endpoints MUST compute the entitlement independently and MUST abort the attempt
if their values differ.

`C` quotes a non-negative `buyout_fee` for its cooperation and a non-negative
`timeout_compensation` payable only through the split path. The **claim** is:

```text
claim_C = entitlement_C + buyout_fee
```

Let `E` be the value of `B`'s CoinJoin change output. `E` is computed by the
round's ordinary tr0 accounting: `B`'s total inputs, including every `cap_k`,
minus its equal output, plus realized maker fee, minus its miner-fee share. The
attempt is feasible only when:

```text
E - claim_C - timeout_compensation - split_fee >= min_split_output
claim_C + timeout_compensation                  >= min_split_output
```

`split_fee` and `min_split_output` are fixed in the accepted proposal.
`min_split_output` MUST be at least 1,000 satoshis so that either split output
can pay for a child fee bump. `B` MUST verify feasibility before committing its
change address to the round and again against the exact unsigned transaction.
`C` MUST verify it against the exact unsigned transaction before signing
anything.

The buyer pays every on-chain cost: the CoinJoin miner-fee share for the channel
inputs and escrow output is ordinary tr0 accounting, the split fee is
subtracted from `B`'s split output, and the sweep fee is paid from `B`'s share
of the escrow. `B` MUST therefore require `E - claim_C - timeout_compensation`
to cover a claim-leaf sweep at its configured worst-case fee rate
(`sweep_fee_reserve`, at least the claim-leaf vsize times 50 sat/vB) in
addition to `min_split_output`; a buyout whose residual cannot fund an urgent
sweep is not feasible.

## Escrow Output

For each attempt, `B` generates a fresh escrow key `K_B` and a fresh claim key
`K_B_claim`; `C` generates a fresh escrow key `K_C` and a fresh 32-byte
`payment_preimage` whose `payment_hash = SHA256(payment_preimage)`. All keys
are compressed secp256k1 public keys, distinct, and never reused. Each party
MUST be able to recover its escrow secrets after data loss, either by
deterministic derivation from its wallet seed or by durably storing them before
any signature is released.

The escrow output is:

```text
internal_key = MuSig2.KeyAgg([K_B, K_C])       (buyer first, then counterparty)
leaf         = OP_SHA256 <payment_hash> OP_EQUALVERIFY <xonly(K_B_claim)> OP_CHECKSIG
tree         = single tapscript leaf (version 0xc0)
output_key   = internal_key + TapTweak(internal_key || tapleaf_hash(leaf))
scriptPubKey = OP_1 <xonly(output_key)>
```

The scriptPubKey is `B`'s change address for the round. To the round peers and
to the chain it is an ordinary P2TR output. It MUST appear exactly once in the
final transaction at exactly the value `E`.

Spends of the escrow output are:

- **Cooperative** key-path spend: both endpoints sign with MuSig2. Used for the
  presigned split and for the post-settlement sweep.
- **Claim** script-path spend: `B` reveals `payment_preimage` and signs with
  `K_B_claim`. It is `B`'s unilateral fallback after settlement.

There is no counterparty-only leaf. `C`'s unilateral path is the presigned
split, which requires no script and does not reveal the leaf.

## Split Transaction

The split is a version 2 transaction with one input and two outputs:

```text
input:   escrow outpoint, nSequence = csv_delay
output0: value = E - claim_C - timeout_compensation - split_fee, script = split_script_B
output1: value = claim_C + timeout_compensation,                 script = split_script_C
nLockTime = 0
```

`csv_delay` is a BIP68 block-based relative lock from 144 through 2016; the
default is 432. It MUST satisfy the settlement inequality of the Settlement
section for the agreed `buyer_settlement_depth` and `cltv_limit`.
`split_script_B` and `split_script_C` are P2TR scripts supplied by their owners
in the proposal and acceptance; each owner MUST be able to spend its output
immediately so that either party can fee-bump the split with a child
transaction. Output order is fixed as shown. `C` MUST compute the actual split
weight and verify that `split_fee` yields at least `split_fee_rate_sat_vb` and
at least the minimum relay fee rate; `B` MUST do the same.

The split is signed with MuSig2 over the tweaked aggregate key using BIP341
`SIGHASH_DEFAULT`. `B` MUST send its partial signature first. `C` MUST verify
the aggregated signature against the escrow output key, durably store the
complete signed split, and only then release any partial signature for the
channel inputs. `C` also returns its split partial so that `B` holds the same
signed split. Neither party MAY broadcast the split before its relative lock
matures; a premature broadcast is invalid by consensus and harmless.

## Parent Signing

The **parent** is the exact unsigned CoinJoin transaction. Its txid does not
depend on witnesses, so both parties derive the escrow outpoint from it. For
each channel input, both parties compute the BIP341 `SIGHASH_DEFAULT` message
over the complete prevout set supplied by `B` and sign with MuSig2 over the
funding output key. `C` MUST verify its own funding outputs in that prevout
set; it need not verify other participants' prevouts, because a signature made
over incorrect prevout data is simply invalid on chain.

Before signing a parent, both parties MUST verify:

- the parent has version 2, a height locktime not more than 12 blocks above
  the verifier's tip or zero, uniform input sequences per JMP-0010, no witness
  or scriptSig data, and no duplicate outpoints;
- every eligible channel funding outpoint appears exactly once as an input,
  and no other input spends a channel of either party;
- the escrow output appears exactly once with the agreed script and value `E`
  satisfying the feasibility inequality; and
- the parent fee rate, computed from `B`'s prevout data and the actual tr0
  weight, is at least `min_parent_fee_rate` from the acceptance and, for `C`,
  at least `C`'s own current estimate for confirmation within
  `parent_wait_blocks`, so that the frozen channel is not tied to a transaction
  that cannot confirm.

Nonces are generated per message with the signer's secret key and the message
bound, used exactly once, and discarded when an attempt fails. A party MUST NOT
reuse a nonce across attempts, parents, or inputs.

`C`'s release of a channel-input partial signature is `C`'s point of no return
for the epoch (see Freeze Epochs). `B` aggregates each channel-input signature,
verifies it against the funding output key, and sends all of its `!sig`
messages for the round together.

## Buyer Integration with tr0

The round peers see only JMP-0001 and JMP-0010 traffic. `B` MUST behave as an
ordinary tr0 participant with these additional obligations:

- `B` MUST complete `buyout_propose`/`buyout_accept` and hold a frozen channel
  before it commits the channel input to a round: before sending `!fill` as a
  taker, or before sending `!ioauth` as a maker. Its change address in that
  message is the escrow scriptPubKey.
- `B` MUST NOT reveal to any round peer that an input is a channel, who `C`
  is, or that the change is an escrow. It MUST NOT select the cofunded ring
  extension (JMP-0014) or any other change-consuming extension for a round in
  which it is a buyer; its change is the escrow and nothing else.
- Freezing a channel per attempt is a griefing surface comparable to ordinary
  UTXO locking during a fill. A maker `B` MUST bound the time a channel stays
  frozen without a parent (`freeze_ttl`), MUST cancel the attempt and release
  the freeze when the round is abandoned before parent signing, and SHOULD
  limit how many times one channel can be frozen per hour.
- A taker `B` builds the parent itself and runs parent signing with `C` before
  or in parallel with `!tx`; it MUST NOT broadcast without complete channel
  signatures. A maker `B` receives `!tx`, runs parent signing with `C`, and
  returns every `!sig` at once. A round peer observes only the ordinary time
  between `!tx` and `!sig`.
- Fidelity bonds are unaffected. A P2WSH bond continues to weight `B`'s tr0
  offers; it is not a transaction input.

Wallet accounting treats the channel as belonging to one mixdepth. The escrow
keys, `split_script_B`, the sweep destination, and the equal output belong to
the same mixdepth as the ordinary change would have.

## Settlement

After the parent confirms, `C` MUST send `buyout_invoice` once the parent
depth reaches `settlement_depth` (from 1 through 144, default 3). The invoice
is a BOLT 11 invoice for exactly `claim_C` with `payment_hash` from the
acceptance and an expiry that leaves `B` enough time to pay under the CLTV rule
below. It MUST carry route hints or another reachability aid when `C` has no
public channels, because the channel that connected `B` and `C` no longer
exists.

Route hints SHOULD name only private channels whose peer is publicly
advertised. A peer with only private channels gains nothing from being named,
and `B` pays a direct peer without a hint. The current joinmarket-ng implementation relies
on LND's private-invoice hint selection, which applies exactly this rule and
does not verify that a usable hint was produced. Its regtest tests settle over
a public route and over a direct private channel, both without payer-supplied
hints.

`B` MUST NOT pay before it has observed the parent at `buyer_settlement_depth`
from the proposal, which MUST be at least `settlement_depth` and at least 3;
the default is 6. A Lightning payment is irreversible, so a reorganization
deeper than `buyer_settlement_depth` after payment would give `C` both the
payment and its channel balance. `buyer_settlement_depth` is the user-selected
finality parameter for that residual risk. `B` MUST continue to monitor the
parent until the escrow is swept and MUST NOT pay while the parent is absent
from its chain view.

Every payment `B` sends MUST carry a CLTV limit `cltv_limit`, the maximum
total CLTV expiry delta the payment may use, fixed in the proposal. The agreed
parameters MUST satisfy:

```text
csv_delay >= buyer_settlement_depth + cltv_limit + sweep_margin
```

`sweep_margin` is at least 36 blocks. In addition, `B` MUST NOT initiate a
payment unless `current_height + cltv_limit <= parent_height + csv_delay -
sweep_margin`. `B` MAY use multi-part payments; all parts share the same
`payment_hash`. `C` MUST settle a payment for the invoice on receipt and MUST
NOT settle for less than `claim_C`.

The current buyer sends one payment part (`max_parts=1`). The optional multi-part
flow above is not an implemented or tested fallback.

Once `B` knows `payment_preimage`, it MUST spend the escrow output before the
split matures, and it MUST be able to do so without `C`. `B` sends
`buyout_sweep` for a cooperative key-path sweep; `C` MUST answer any sweep of a
settled escrow that pays only `B`-chosen P2TR outputs. If `B` does not hold a
complete cooperative sweep signature within `sweep_response_blocks` (from the
proposal, at most 6) after settlement, or if a broadcast cooperative sweep is
still unconfirmed at `parent_height + csv_delay - sweep_margin`, `B` MUST
broadcast a claim-leaf sweep. Both sweeps MUST spend the whole escrow output,
MUST signal replaceability, and MUST be monitored and fee-bumped by `B` until
confirmed. The claim-leaf sweep MUST be affordable from `B`'s share at
`sweep_fee_reserve`. A settled escrow that remains unspent when the relative
lock matures lets `C` collect `claim_C + timeout_compensation` a second time,
and `C` MAY do so; the rules above exist so that a compliant `B` never reaches
that state.

Recovery after restart MUST NOT require acceptance of an earlier rejected or
evicted sweep before constructing a higher-fee replacement. A saved broadcast
intent permits replay, but repeated fee-policy rejection must not prevent fee
escalation within the operator's budget. Replacing a cooperative sweep with a
claim-leaf sweep exposes the script path; implementations should retain an
accepted cooperative sweep until the safety deadline where feasible.

If no invoice is paid, `C` broadcasts the signed split when the relative lock
matures and MAY fee-bump it through its own output. `B` MAY do the same. Both
parties SHOULD prefer Lightning settlement: a cooperative key-path sweep is an
ordinary P2TR spend, whereas the split reveals two related outputs and the
claim leaf reveals a hash-locked script path.

## Freeze Epochs, Cancellation, and Recovery

A **freeze epoch** begins when `C` accepts a proposal and the channel is
quiesced. Within an epoch the commitment balances, the entitlement, and
therefore `claim_C` are fixed. An epoch ends only when one of these is
confirmed on chain: a parent, a cooperative close, or a commitment
transaction. Both parties MUST persist the epoch, its frozen balances, and the
channel's exact state before acknowledging the freeze.

An epoch contains one or more **attempts**, numbered from 0. At most one
attempt is active at a time; a new attempt MUST NOT be proposed while another
is in any state before `dormant`. Each attempt has fresh escrow keys, a fresh
payment hash, a fresh escrow script, and fresh nonces. An attempt moves through
these states, each persisted before the message that leaves it is sent:

| State | Entered when | Leaves by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `accepted` | `C` sent `buyout_accept` | `buyout_parent`, cancel |
| `parent_received` | `C` verified `buyout_parent` and sent `buyout_nonces` | `buyout_split_partial`, cancel |
| `split_signed` | `C` holds the complete signed split | `buyout_parent_partials`, cancel |
| `parent_signed` | `C` released channel-input partials (point of no return) | chain observation, `dormant` |
| `dormant` | parent unseen in mempool and chain for `parent_wait_blocks` after `parent_signed` | chain observation only |
| `confirmed` | parent at `settlement_depth` in the observer's chain view | `buyout_invoice` |
| `invoice_sent` | `C` sent `buyout_invoice` | payment or split maturity |
| `settled` | `C` settled the payment / `B` holds the preimage | sweep confirmed or split confirmed |
| `swept` | a sweep of the escrow confirmed | terminal |
| `split_broadcast` | the split confirmed | terminal |
| `superseded` | another attempt's parent, a cooperative close, or a commitment transaction confirmed | terminal |
| `canceled` | `buyout_cancel` accepted before `parent_signed` | terminal |

`buyout_cancel` MUST be rejected at or after `parent_signed`. A `dormant`
attempt is not dead: its parent signature remains valid until the funding
output is spent, so both parties MUST retain its signed split and MUST keep
watching for its parent until the epoch ends. If a dormant or any earlier
attempt's parent later appears, that attempt resumes at `confirmed` and every
other attempt becomes `superseded`. `parent_wait_blocks` comes from the
acceptance and is at least 6.

If no channel-input partial signature has ever been released in the epoch, a
cancel also ends the epoch and both parties resume the channel. After any such
release, the channel MUST NOT resume normal operation, because a complete
signature for a parent that spends the funding output may exist and could be
published later against a changed balance. The parties then have exactly these
options:

1. Wait for the active or a dormant parent to confirm. A party that holds the
   exact final transaction SHOULD rebroadcast it while it is absent from the
   mempool; a maker `B` normally does not hold it, because only the taker
   assembles all signatures.
2. Once the active attempt is `dormant`, start a new attempt under the same
   epoch with a new round. This is safe because the frozen entitlement is
   unchanged and every attempt has its own escrow and split.
3. End the epoch by closing the channel. `C` MAY request a cooperative close
   with `buyout_close` once `max_freeze_blocks` (from the acceptance, at least
   144) have passed since the freeze without a confirmed parent; `B` MUST
   cooperate, and the close MUST pay each party its frozen commitment balance.
   Either party MAY force-close at any time; a confirmed commitment or close
   transaction invalidates every parent of the epoch.

A party that cannot reconstruct its state safely MUST refuse new attempts on
that channel and MUST NOT resume the channel. Restart reconciliation MUST
re-derive every attempt's escrow script and split from durable state, check the
funding outpoint and any known escrow outpoint against its own chain view, and
continue from the observed state. Peer claims about chain state are not
evidence. `buyout_status` exists to resynchronize message state after a
restart, not to replace chain observation.

## Transport and Encoding

All messages travel over the BOLT 8 peer connection between `B` and `C` as
BOLT 1 custom messages of type `53357`. The connection authenticates both node
identities; no additional payload signature is used. A receiver MUST bind every
message to the peer's node ID and MUST reject a message about an epoch or
attempt that peer does not own. The Lightning connection is independent of the
channel being spent, so the parties remain reachable after the parent confirms.

The custom message payload is `canonical_json(payload)` per RFC 8785 (UTF-8
JSON Canonicalization Scheme). Schemas use integers for satoshis, heights, and
counters; lowercase hex without `0x`; no `null`; no duplicate keys; and no
unrecognized fields. A payload MUST be at most 65,000 bytes. Satoshi values are
0 through 2,100,000,000,000,000; heights and counters are 0 through
4,294,967,295. Hashes, preimages, and IDs are exactly 64 hex characters;
compressed public keys are 66; MuSig2 public nonces are 132; partial signatures
are 64; BIP340 signatures are 128; scriptPubKeys are at most 68 (P2TR only);
transactions are hex and at most 120,000 characters. A parser MUST reject a
payload outside these bounds before allocating nested structures.

Every payload contains `v` (exactly `1`), `type`, `epoch_id` (fresh random
`hex32` chosen by `B`), and `attempt`. Hashes over objects use:

```text
H(kind, object) = SHA256(UTF8("JMP0011/" + kind + "/v1\u0000") || canonical_json(object))
```

`proposal_hash = H("proposal", buyout_propose)` and
`accept_hash = H("accept", buyout_accept)` are the identifiers of an attempt's
terms; every later message of the attempt carries `accept_hash`.

## Messages

| Type | Direction | Type-specific fields |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `buyout_propose` | B to C | `network`, `channel_points[]`, `K_B`, `K_B_claim`, `split_script_B`, `csv_delay`, `split_fee_rate_sat_vb`, `split_fee`, `min_split_output`, `sweep_fee_reserve`, `buyer_settlement_depth`, `cltv_limit`, `sweep_response_blocks`, `max_buyout_fee`, `max_timeout_compensation`, `freeze_ttl_blocks`, `expiry` |
| `buyout_accept` | C to B | `proposal_hash`, `K_C`, `payment_hash`, `split_script_C`, `entitlement_C`, `buyout_fee`, `timeout_compensation`, `min_parent_fee_rate_sat_vb`, `parent_wait_blocks`, `settlement_depth`, `max_freeze_blocks`, `frozen_state_hash` |
| `buyout_reject` | C to B | `proposal_hash`, `reason_code` |
| `buyout_parent` | B to C | `accept_hash`, `unsigned_parent_tx`, `prevouts[]`, `escrow_output_index`, `channel_input_indices[]`, `split_nonce_B`, `parent_nonces_B[]` |
| `buyout_nonces` | C to B | `accept_hash`, `parent_hash`, `split_nonce_C`, `parent_nonces_C[]` |
| `buyout_split_partial` | B to C | `accept_hash`, `parent_hash`, `split_partial_B` |
| `buyout_parent_partials` | C to B | `accept_hash`, `parent_hash`, `split_partial_C`, `parent_partials_C[]` |
| `buyout_invoice` | C to B | `accept_hash`, `txid`, `bolt11` |
| `buyout_sweep` | B to C | `accept_hash`, `unsigned_sweep_tx`, `sweep_nonce_B` |
| `buyout_sweep_partial` | C to B | `accept_hash`, `sweep_hash`, `sweep_nonce_C`, `sweep_partial_C` |
| `buyout_cancel` | either | `accept_hash` or `proposal_hash`, `reason_code` |
| `buyout_close` | either | `reason_code` |
| `buyout_status` | either | `accept_hash`, `stage`, optionally both `parent_hash` and `txid` as specified below |

`channel_points[]` lists objects with exactly `txid:hex32` and `vout:uint32`,
all with counterparty `C`. `prevouts[]` gives `value` and `script_pubkey` for every parent input in
order. `parent_hash` is the single SHA256 of the parent's consensus
serialization without witness; `txid` is the display-order double-SHA256.
`frozen_state_hash` is `H("frozen-state", ...)` over the channel points, both
commitment balances, the initiator flags, commitment fee, and anchor values, so
that `B` can detect a disagreement about the frozen state before the round
starts. `reason_code` matches `^[a-z0-9_]+$` with 1 through 64 bytes. `stage`
is one of the attempt states of the Freeze Epochs section. A status for
`accepted` MUST omit both parent identifiers. States from `parent_received`
through settlement MUST include both. A `canceled` or `superseded` attempt
includes both only if that attempt had received a parent. One identifier
without the other, null identifiers, and placeholder hashes are invalid.

Channel-point, input-index, nonce, and partial-signature arrays have 1 through
32 entries; `prevouts` has 1 through 252. Channel points and input indices
MUST be unique; indices MUST address the supplied prevout list, and the buyer's
nonce array MUST have the same length as its channel-input-index array. A
runtime MUST also check these arrays against the frozen channel set and the
counterparty's responses before signing. Payload size may further restrict
the number of entries that can be sent.

A message out of order, for an unknown epoch or attempt, with an unexpected
`accept_hash`, or after a terminal stage MUST be rejected. An exact duplicate
MAY receive the durably cached response; a conflicting duplicate MUST abort the
attempt while cancellation is still possible.

## Backend Requirements

An endpoint's Lightning backend MUST be able to:

- quiesce a private Taproot channel with no pending HTLCs, report its exact
  commitment balances, initiator role, commitment fee, and anchor values, and
  keep the channel frozen across restarts until explicitly released;
- refuse cooperative close, splice, and normal channel updates while frozen,
  except for a close requested through `buyout_close` or a force-close;
- produce MuSig2 public nonces and partial signatures for a caller-supplied
  spend of the funding output, bound to the complete BIP341 prevout set, and
  verify the counterparty's partial signature; and
- persist every released partial signature and the exact parent it authorizes.

Stock backends do not expose these operations; the joinmarket-ng reference uses
a patched LND that adds them as a scoped RPC service. An endpoint MUST NOT
answer `buyout_propose` unless its backend implements all of them. There is no
handshake or peerlist capability; support is discovered by sending
`buyout_propose`; a peer without support ignores the
odd-typed custom message, and `B` treats the absence of an answer within
`expiry` as a rejection.

## Compatibility

Round peers require no change: a buyout round is a valid JMP-0010 round. The
extension is invisible to the orderbook, directory servers, and the taker's
maker selection. `B` and `C` need matching backend support and a live peer
connection. A peer that does not support the extension is simply never asked
to freeze anything. JMP-0001 and JMP-0010 message schemas are unchanged.

A buyer MUST NOT participate in a JMP-0014 cofunded ring round as a buyer,
because the ring consumes all change and the escrow is the buyer's change.
Other participants of a buyout round are unaffected and unaware.

After a successful payment, the current buyer attempts an immediate
cooperative key-path sweep, with a unilateral claim fallback. It does not yet
retain a paid escrow for a later CoinJoin input. Such an opt-in spend needs a
separate multi-input cooperative signing contract and a deadline-safe fallback;
the existing one-input sweep signer must not be interpreted as that feature.

## Privacy and Security Considerations

The counterparty necessarily learns the complete parent, including `B`'s
ordinary inputs and its escrow output, and therefore learns which change
belongs to `B` and that `B`'s inputs are co-spent. It does not learn which
equal output is `B`'s. `B` SHOULD minimize its ordinary inputs and SHOULD treat
`C` as it treats any Lightning peer that already knows the channel's funding
outpoint and its node identity.

Round peers see one more P2TR key-path input and one more P2TR change output.
The only side channel is the time `B` takes between `!tx` and `!sig`, which
includes two round trips to `C`. Implementations SHOULD keep that latency
within the normal spread of maker response times and MUST NOT send channel
input signatures separately from ordinary ones.

`C`'s safety rests on holding the complete signed split before releasing any
parent partial, on the split fee being sufficient or fee-bumpable, and on
never resuming the channel after a partial is released. `B`'s safety rests on
paying only after adequate confirmation and on sweeping before the relative
lock matures. The CLTV inequality guarantees that a payment cannot remain in
flight past the point where `B` could no longer sweep in time.

A reorganization that removes the parent after `B` paid the invoice would give
`C` both the payment and its channel balance. `buyer_settlement_depth` bounds
that risk and is `B`'s choice; `settlement_depth` only schedules `C`'s invoice.
`C`'s corresponding exposure is a parent that never confirms while its channel
stays frozen; `parent_wait_blocks`, `max_freeze_blocks`, and the fee checks at
release bound that exposure to a cooperative or forced close at the frozen
balances, never to a loss of the entitlement.

The split and the claim leaf are distinguishable on chain and reveal the escrow
relationship. They are fallbacks; the intended path is Lightning settlement
followed by a cooperative key-path sweep, which leaves the escrow output
indistinguishable from ordinary change spent by its owner.

A node's incoming and outgoing channels in one co-funded ring (JMP-0014) share
a funding transaction. If the node bought out both, its two escrow outputs and
later sweeps would tie both edges to one owner and expose part of the ring. A
buyer therefore MUST NOT start a buyout of a channel whose funding transaction
also funds a channel it already bought out or is buying out, unless the earlier
attempt was affirmatively canceled before any signature. Each party MUST use a
fresh `split_script` for every session and MUST NOT reuse it after cancellation,
so that no two buyouts pay one address. The rule is only as strong as the
record it is checked against, so an implementation MUST keep all buyer sessions
of one LN node in one durable buyer journal (or enforce the equivalent rule
across journals).

This construction hides one channel close inside a CoinJoin; it does not by
itself provide an anonymity gain for the buyer's later spends, and it does not
protect against a counterparty that reveals the round to third parties.
Fidelity-bond-weighted maker selection and PoDLE continue to apply unchanged.

## Reference Implementation

- [joinmarket-ng](https://github.com/joinmarket-ng/joinmarket-ng), Taproot pit
  branch: `jmswap` escrow primitives (escrow tree, split construction and
  verification, MuSig2 sessions), buyer runtime in `taker` and `maker`,
  counterparty daemon, and patched LND `ChannelEscrow` service. Regtest
  coverage exercises the cooperative path, the claim leaf, the split, restart
  reconciliation, and force-close during a frozen epoch.

## Non-Goals

This specification does not define the patched backend's RPC surface, key
derivation paths, wallet allocation policy, fee selection beyond the stated
minimums, Lightning routing strategy, channel rings or cofunding (JMP-0014),
submarine swaps, or any change to JMP-0001 or JMP-0010 message flows.
